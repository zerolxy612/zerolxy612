## Design Context

### Users

The primary visitors are frontend and full-stack hiring managers, engineers reviewing a candidate's work, and open-source maintainers. They typically scan the profile for 15–30 seconds and need to identify the developer's role, strongest work, engineering judgment, and credible contribution evidence without decoding decorative elements.

### Brand Personality

Precise, inventive, and credible. The profile should feel technically curious and visually distinctive while remaining calm enough for professional evaluation. Confidence should come from shipped work, tests, documentation, and direct links rather than inflated claims.

### Aesthetic Direction

A restrained technical-editorial identity: dark warm neutrals, off-white typography, muted teal and coral accents, asymmetric composition, and a small amount of system-diagram language. Avoid neon cyan-purple gradients, glowing effects, animated typing, badge walls, centered layouts, and repeated contribution visualizations.

### Design Principles

1. Put evidence before decoration: featured work and merged contributions appear before activity graphics.
2. Make the first 15 seconds useful: role, product focus, strongest project, and primary links must be immediately scannable.
3. Keep every claim verifiable through a source, demo, pull request, release, test, or document.
4. Use progressive detail: a concise profile summary links to deeper repository documentation.
5. Preserve accessibility and resilience: meaningful alt text, readable contrast, reduced motion, mobile-safe layouts, and useful content even when an external image fails.
